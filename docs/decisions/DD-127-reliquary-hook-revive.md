# DD-127: Reliquary (plan item C7; designer brief 2026-10-09) ships as a one-slot, level-12 PLAYTEST item: the first piece its owner loses this battle returns to its starting square at the end of the opponent's next turn, if that square is empty; one payout, spent whether the return succeeds or fizzles, and left armed while the piece is back on the board by other means (Rebirth)

Status: binding under D-37 (designer may overrule).
Spec: 5.2, 5.4, 7.2, 8.2, R-LOAD-002, R-INFO-002, R-SEC-001, INV-03, R-ELEM-005, D-39, plan item C7

## Decision

Reliquary (plan item C7; designer brief 2026-10-09) ships as a one-slot, level-12 PLAYTEST item: the first piece its owner loses this battle returns to its starting square at the end of the opponent's next turn, if that square is empty; one payout, spent whether the return succeeds or fizzles, and left armed while the piece is back on the board by other means (Rebirth). It needs a hook-level revive, the new MutCtx.revive(piece, to) returning 'revived', 'fizzled' or 'unavailable': the same intercepts as a REVIVE effect (INV-03 for the acting player's king, Hot Foot's burning squares, item intercepts), PieceRevived with the item as source, EffectFizzled with ability null and a new item field, and 'unavailable' on the throwaway drafts that legal-move generation runs onTurnEnd on, where the hook changes nothing. Warden's Stopwatch (version 2) also stops an item's revive through a new effectIntercept with the new fizzle reason negated and is revealed as the source; the projection hides an item's fizzle from the opponent until the item is known, and the Reliquary reveals itself when a piece returns. The slice is private: a projected slice keyed by the item's id would tell the opponent the item is worn (R-SEC-001), so the NPC infers an unused payout from a side that has lost no piece yet. Spec 7.2 updated (the Stopwatch row too), content guide 4.10 and 7.2.

## Why it is the best case

The brief's wording ('the first piece you lose', 'at the end of your opponent's turn, if empty', 'one insurance payout; Warden's Stopwatch negates it') fixes the timing and the brake. Items had no way to put a piece on the board, and running a REVIVE through the existing intercepts is the smallest capability that keeps INV-03, Hot Foot and the Stopwatch in force without a second resolver. Spending the payout on a fizzled attempt keeps the item one-shot and deterministic; leaving it armed while the piece is back by Rebirth avoids wasting it on a piece that did not need it. The Stopwatch's reach is extended rather than the Reliquary checking for it by id, so any future item revive is covered the same way, which is what D-39 intends.
