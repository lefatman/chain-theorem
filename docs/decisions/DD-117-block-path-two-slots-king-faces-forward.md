# DD-117: Block Path costs two ability slots (slotCost 2, version 2) and a king always faces forward after it moves: in the onActionEnd hook a moved king is never asked the facing question; its facing entry is deleted so it faces the owner's forward direction (the default of facingOf/defaultFacing), and a FacingSet event with the forward facing is emitted only when the facing actually changed (an entry already equal to forward is dropped silently; a king with no entry produces no event)

Status: binding under D-37 (designer may overrule).
Spec: 5.4, 5.7, 5.8, 5.6, R-ABIL-005, R-RULES-005, R-SEC-001, INV-03, DD-99, DD-102, DD-105, plan item B5

## Decision

Block Path costs two ability slots (slotCost 2, version 2) and a king always faces forward after it moves: in the onActionEnd hook a moved king is never asked the facing question; its facing entry is deleted so it faces the owner's forward direction (the default of facingOf/defaultFacing), and a FacingSet event with the forward facing is emitted only when the facing actually changed (an entry already equal to forward is dropped silently; a king with no entry produces no event). Every other piece keeps the declinable prompt and the kingSafeAfter filter that only served king prompts is gone. Kings remain included in the rule (a king is not in check from its facing, DD-99), so a king is guarded from the front only; castling asks for the rook and not the king. The rules text reads 'the king always faces forward after it moves'. Spec 5.7 and 5.8 rows updated and one sentence appended to DD-99; tests: module data expects 2 slots, 'R-ABIL-005 DD-99 a king that moves faces forward again without a prompt, so it is checked from the side it had been facing' (a seeded east facing, since no prompt can set one any more) and a castling case added.

## Why it is the best case

docs/BALANCE_BASELINE.md section 5 has Block Path at 50.0% on its own in First Blood (36 games) but 72.9% in Full Battle (24 games, median 88 plies), above the 60% bar in the long format where a facing has time to matter. Its real weight is in the king set: finding 3 shows Maximum and Flexible carrying Stalwart, Block Path and Veil (plus Electric Slide) on the king, 'a king that cannot be checkmated, is not in check from one direction and whose abilities act unnamed', beating Focused 79% and 85% in Full Battle and Starter 96-100%, with the only losses being a move capture of the king (stalwart_captured 12 of 144); section 9.3 finding 2 repeats that with a paid Stalwart king the Schedule builds beat Focused 81-88% and names 'at most two passives per set takes Block Path or Veil off the king' as a B5 lever. A king that always faces forward loses the 'not in check from one direction' half of that set on any king that has moved, which is the half that turned a Stalwart king into an uncatchable one, while a stationary king keeps its forward guard and ordinary pieces keep the full ability. Two slots prices the card in line with Obstinate for the same reason: a passive that removes legal captures should not ride for free beside three reaction cards. The forward reset also retires the only place a passive had to consult kingSafeAfter (INV-03) for a prompt, since a king facing can no longer be turned away from an attacker.
